Families

Each family is shown through its founding element: the genome copy its first internal model was taken from. The verdict is computed from the evidence below it, as a triage aid. It is not a curation call.

Primer tRNAs

How these were measured

Structure (TSD, end motif, PBS, PPT, LTR identity) comes from ltr-forage's candidate table. The TSD is the duplicated sequence just outside both LTRs: an exact repeat, or a 5–6 bp repeat with one mismatch. The primer binding site is ltr-forage's search for the 3′ end of one of the genome's own tRNA genes (ARAGORN's method) just inside the 5′ LTR, on either strand. The PPT is the most purine-rich 15 bp window in the 30 bp before the 3′ LTR; 11 or more purines is suggestive.

Domains come from hmmscan of six-frame ORFs of at least 60 aa against the TE protein profiles; overlapping weaker hits are dropped. Domains on both strands of one element usually mean one element nested in another.

Copies. Full-length copies are the elements ltr-forage found and clustered into this family, and the sequences they lie on. When the genome was masked with the library, near-full-length copies are hits covering at least half of a model.

Verdicts. Possible artifact: domains on both strands, a segmental-duplication flag, or two or three copies all on one sequence with few genome copies elsewhere. Known family: the LTR or internal region is at least 80% identical over 80% of its length to a reference family. Likely novel (or well supported without a reference): three lines of evidence among two or more protein domains, a TSD of 4 bp or longer, TG…CA ends, a PBS, full-length copies on several sequences and ten or more near-full-length genome copies; or two of them when one is two or more domains. Elements without domains can qualify: non-autonomous elements keep the structure but lose the coding region. Everything else is weak evidence.
